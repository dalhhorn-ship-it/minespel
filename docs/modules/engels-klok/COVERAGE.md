# Engels: hoe laat is het?: where the content comes from

Source: a topic from the founder on 2026-11-15: "maak een leermodule over Engels klok lezen en uitleg, aangezien dit anders werkt t.o.v. Nederlands." There are no photos and no school sheet. All lessons and questions are newly written.

`engels-runaway/klok.json` already teaches o'clock, half past and a quarter past or to, for groep 5 and 6. This module goes deeper and shares no items or sentences with it.

| Part of the topic | Topic | Skills | Question styles |
|---|---|---|---|
| A full hour and half past, and why Dutch half is different | `uur-en-half` | `oclock`, `half-past`, `half-valkuil` | time to English, English to time, Dutch half to English and back, type the missing word or number, type the whole time, find the wrong word (some with no mistake) |
| Quarter past and quarter to | `kwart` | `quarter-past`, `quarter-to` | time to English, English to time, type with and without "a", type the whole time, find the wrong or misspelled word |
| Minutes past and to, Dutch "vijf voor half" and "tien over half", other minutes | `minuten` | `minuten-past`, `minuten-to`, `rond-half`, `andere-minuten` | time to English, Dutch to English in steps, English to Dutch, type the missing number, find the wrong word |
| The digital way, a.m. and p.m., no 24 hour clock | `digitaal` | `digitaal`, `am-pm`, `24-uur` | say it like a digital clock, pick a.m. or p.m., 24 hour time to p.m., type, find the wrong word |
| Asking and saying the time | `vragen` | `what-time`, `at-time`, `time-for` | pick the right question or answer, fill in time, at, for or to, find the wrong word |

The big trap gets its own lesson page (`half-valkuil`) and 18 items, plus more in the other topics: Dutch "half drie" looks forward to three, English "half past two" looks back at two. The lesson gives a rule both ways: Dutch to English is the number minus one, English to Dutch is plus one.

The page `rond-half` teaches a step by step way for Dutch times that English does not have: first make digits (vijf voor half drie is 2:25), then ask "before or after half?", then say past with the old hour or to with the new hour.

## Levels

`["M6", "E6", "M7"]`, for groep 6 and 7.
* M6: o'clock, half past, a quarter past and to, five and ten past and to, a.m. and p.m., the question What time is it? Mostly choice, a few one word gaps.
* E6: the half trap in both directions, twenty-five past and to, vijf voor half and tien over half, oh in 7:05, 24 hour to p.m., at and time for. More typing and find the error.
* M7: whole times typed in English and in Dutch, other minutes with "minutes" (seven minutes past three), times with p.m. in digits, time for versus time to, a question with does.

"Other minutes" is only at M7, as the founder asked. No E7: the topic does not need a fourth step.

## Items

136 items, 26 to 28 per topic, 8 to 10 per level per topic. 65 choice, 43 type, 28 find-error (12 of them with no mistake).

Every item with an English sentence and a gap shows the Dutch meaning in its prompt (`Het betekent: ...`, decision 102).

Typed answers accept a lower case and a capital first letter, the straight ' and the curly ’ apostrophe, "a quarter" and "quarter", "twenty-five" with and without the hyphen (also forty-five and thirty-five), and It's or It is. Times with p.m. accept p.m., pm, P.M. and PM, with or without a space. The Dutch answer for twenty past ten accepts "tien voor half elf" and "twintig over tien".

## Exams

All exams use all three levels, pass at 70 percent and give one minute per question.
* Toets Heel uur, half en kwart: 12 questions, half weighs double because of the trap.
* Toets Minuten over en voor: 10 questions.
* Toets Digitaal en vragen: 10 questions.
* Toets Hoe laat is het?: 25 questions over all topics, uur-en-half and minuten weigh double.

## Choices a teacher may want to check

* School English is taught: "a quarter past" (with "a" is the common British school form, without is accepted) and "seven minutes past three" (with "minutes"). "seven past three" counts as wrong, as most school books teach.
* "half four" (British slang for 4:30) is never used, so it cannot confuse with Dutch half vier.
* No questions on 12 a.m. and 12 p.m.; the lesson says people often say noon.

The content still needs one check by a teacher or parent (AC124).
